MacBook Air Liquid Cooling

Designed and modified a 2017 MacBook Air, replacing its stock fan-based cooling system with a custom liquid-cooling solution.

PROJECT TYPE Personal Project
CATEGORY Hardware Modification
FOCUS Thermal Management
OVERVIEW

Rethinking the cooling system.

This project explored the possibility of replacing the stock cooling solution of a 2017 MacBook Air with a custom liquid-cooling system.

Rather than simply relying on the original fan-based thermal management system, I investigated how a liquid-cooling solution could be integrated into the compact architecture of the laptop.

DESIGN

Designing around constraints.

One of the biggest challenges was the extremely limited internal space of the MacBook Air.

Every component of the cooling system had to be considered in relation to the existing motherboard, chassis, battery, and other internal components.

DESIGN PRIORITY Maximize thermal transfer while working within the physical constraints of the laptop.
DESIGN PROCESS

From concept to a working cooling loop.

The goal was to create a compact liquid-cooling loop in which each component served a specific role while fitting within the limited space available inside the MacBook Air.

01 / EXPLODED VIEW

Understanding the system.

02 / SYSTEM OPERATION

How the cooling loop works.

The system begins at the copper cold plate, which provides the thermal interface between the processor and the coolant.

As the processor generates heat, the copper plate transfers that heat into the circulating coolant. The heated coolant is then pumped away from the cold plate and toward the dedicated cooling section.

The cooling section consists of a fan and a custom metal cooling tube. Heat is transferred from the coolant through the tube and dissipated into the surrounding air with the assistance of the fan.

Once the coolant has been cooled, it returns to the reservoir. From there, the pump circulates it back toward the cold plate, completing the cycle.

03 / COMPONENT BREAKDOWN

Inside the system.

Each component was designed around a specific function within the cooling loop.

01
HEAT TRANSFER

Copper Cold Plate

The copper plate acts as the primary thermal interface between the processor and the coolant.

Copper was selected for its high thermal conductivity, allowing heat generated by the processor to transfer efficiently into the circulating coolant.

02
STRUCTURAL

3D-Printed Enclosure

The copper cold plate is bonded to a custom enclosure fabricated through 3D printing.

The enclosure provides the structure needed to integrate the cold plate with the rest of the cooling loop while accommodating the limited space available inside the laptop.

03
CIRCULATION

Pump

The pump drives the coolant through the system, moving heated coolant away from the cold plate and toward the cooling section.

04
HEAT DISSIPATION

Cooling Tube

The custom metal tube acts as the primary heat-exchange component in the cooling section.

Heat from the coolant is transferred through the tube and dissipated into the surrounding air with the assistance of the fan.

05
AIRFLOW

Cooling Fan

The fan increases airflow across the cooling tube, helping transfer heat from the coolant into the surrounding environment.

06
COOLANT MANAGEMENT

Reservoir

The reservoir stores the coolant and provides a central point for the coolant to return to after passing through the cooling section.

FROM DESIGN TO HARDWARE

The concept was ready. Now it was time to build it.

With the function and placement of each component established, the next challenge was turning the concept into a physical system.

IMPLEMENTATION

Turning the design into hardware.

After developing the cooling concept, the system was physically integrated into the MacBook Air.

The modification required careful handling of the laptop's internal components and precise placement of the cooling hardware.

HANDS-ON EXPERIENCE

This stage of the project provided hands-on experience with hardware modification, mechanical constraints, prototyping, and thermal management.

RESULTS

What I learned.

This project gave me a practical understanding of the challenges involved in designing thermal systems for compact electronics.

More importantly, it reinforced the importance of designing around real-world constraints. A technically effective solution still needs to physically fit, interact safely with existing components, and remain practical to implement.
