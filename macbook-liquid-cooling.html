01 · HARDWARE MODIFICATION

MacBook Air
Liquid Cooling

Designed and modified a 2017 MacBook Air, replacing its stock fan-based cooling system with a custom liquid-cooling solution.

PROJECT TYPE Group Project
CATEGORY Hardware Modification
FOCUS Thermal Management
OVERVIEW

Rethinking the cooling system.

This project explored the possibility of replacing the stock cooling solution of a 2017 MacBook Air with a custom liquid-cooling system.

Rather than simply using the original fan-based thermal management system, I investigated how a liquid-cooling solution could be integrated into the compact architecture of the laptop.

DESIGN PROCESS

From concept to a working cooling loop.

The goal was to replace the MacBook Air's original fan-based cooling system with a compact liquid cooling loop. The design process focused on how each component could work together while fitting within the constraints of the laptop.

Understanding the system.

Initial design
SYSTEM OPERATION

How the cooling loop works.

The system begins at the copper cold plate, which provides the thermal interface between the processor and the coolant. As the processor generates heat, the copper plate transfers that heat into the circulating coolant.

The heated coolant is then pumped away from the cold plate and into a dedicated cooling section. This section uses a custom metal cooling tube together with a fan to transfer heat from the coolant into the surrounding air.

After passing through the cooling section, the coolant returns to the reservoir. From there, it is pumped back toward the cold plate, creating a continuous cooling loop.

COMPONENT BREAKDOWN

Inside the system.

Each component was designed around a specific function within the cooling loop.

01
HEAT TRANSFER

Copper Cold Plate & 3D-Printed Enclosure

The copper cold plate serves as the primary thermal interface between the processor and the coolant. Its high thermal conductivity allows heat generated by the processor to efficiently transfer into the circulating coolant.

The cold plate is bonded to a custom 3D-printed enclosure, which provides the structural support needed to integrate it into the cooling loop while accommodating the limited space available inside the MacBook Air.

02
CIRCULATION

Pump

The pump drives the coolant through the system, moving heated coolant away from the cold plate and toward the cooling section.

03
HEAT DISSIPATION

Cooling System

The custom metal tube acts as the primary heat-exchange component in the cooling section.

Heat from the coolant is transferred through the tube and dissipated into the surrounding air with the assistance of the fan.

The fan increases airflow across the cooling tube, helping transfer heat from the coolant into the surrounding environment.

IMPLEMENTATION

Once the system was designed, it was time to build it.

STEP 01

Component Fabrication

After developing the cooling concept, the components were fabricated using 3D printing. The cooling tube was produced using a specialized metal 3D-printing process. Titanium powder was first deposited onto the build platform, after which a laser selectively melted the powder layer by layer according to the geometry of the cooling tube. Once the printing was complete, the excess titanium powder was removed, leaving the finished cooling tube.

STEP 02

Cooling Hardware Installation

Once all of the components were fabricated, we began assembling the cooling system inside the MacBook. Each component was carefully positioned to fit within the laptop's limited internal space while maintaining proper connections between the cooling hardware.

STEP 03

Leak Testing & Sealing

Before connecting the system to the MacBook, we filled the cooling loop with coolant and inspected it for leaks. Any leakage points were carefully sealed with silicone to ensure the system was properly contained before operation.

STEP 04

Electrical Integration

Finally, we connected the pump and fan to the MacBook's power system. This step required extra care to ensure the electrical connections were secure and properly integrated. Once the MacBook was powered on, the cooling system activated alongside the computer.

Conclusion and Final RESULTS

What I learned.

This project gave me a practical understanding of the challenges involved in designing thermal systems for compact electronics.

More importantly, it reinforced the importance of designing around real-world constraints. A technically effective solution still needs to physically fit, interact safely with existing components, and remain practical to implement.
